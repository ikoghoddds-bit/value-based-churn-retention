The framework

The business question comes first: whom should the retention team call, and what can it afford to spend? A simple calibrated logistic regression identifies risk. The list ranks every customer by P(churn) × monthly customer value. A risk flag is shown separately from the dollar rank.

The primary project follows the public-data/monthly-value walkthrough. A separate synthetic pension extension preserves earlier member-transfer tasks and distinguishes assets from fee revenue.

Recall first, with a visible cost

The highest validation threshold achieving at least 85% recall is 0.24. Held-out recall is 82.9%, compared with 53.5% at threshold 0.50. It catches 310 of 374 churners and flags 330 staying customers; precision is 48.4%.

At illustrative penalties of $20 per unnecessary contact and $100 per missed churner, screening cost is $13,000 versus $19,840 at 0.50. These are diagnostic penalties, not realized campaign costs or profit. Validation-only cost-minimizing comparisons and multiple ratios make the assumption testable.

How much retention spend is justified?

Break-even total spend = p × monthly value × retained months × save rate × contribution margin. Subtract outreach cost for the remaining incentive cap. Stay below break-even for positive expected contribution.

Example: risk 50%, monthly value $100, six retained months, 25% save rate, 70% margin, $5 outreach. Monthly exposure is $50; total break-even spend is $52.50; incentive cap is $47.50. A $35 advisor cost plus $5 outreach leaves $12.50 modeled contribution.

Advisor, promo and outreach-only tiers are affordability illustrations, not proven optimal treatments. The separate $20 all-in campaign scenario must not also charge these tier costs.

Simple model, inspectable evidence

Public sample, stratified 60/20/20 split, one logistic model and a prior baseline. Preprocessing stays inside training-only calibration folds. Recall and cost thresholds use validation data; test data evaluate the frozen choices. No complex model competition.

Business recommendations

Run a controlled pilot among high-exposure positive-value accounts. Use spending caps as upper bounds and prefer cheaper proven-effective support. Randomize outreach versus control, record contact and incentive costs, and evaluate incremental retained contribution. Monitor calibration, recall, outreach volume, subgroup errors and realized economics.

The default uniform-cost scenario—25% save rate, six months, 70% margin, $20/contact and 200 contacts—estimates $17,781 saved revenue and $8,447 net contribution. The treatment effect remains assumed.
