Who might leave the pension fund—and how much would they take?

Calibrated 90-day member churn predictions connected to individual transfer exposure, fee-revenue risk and a retention decision.

Synthetic demonstration. All 14,400 member records and outcomes are fabricated by a transparent generator. Results do not describe a real fund or achieved retention.

Goal: identify potential leavers and support retention

Task 1: List pension members who might leave within 90 days, using a calibrated probability and a validation-selected flag.

Task 2: Attach how much each could take if they leave, then calculate probability-weighted outflow and the separate fee revenue at risk.

The dashboard also supports retention planning. Whether outreach actually prevents departure must be tested with a randomized pilot.

Default full-balance transfer: amount if leaving = account balance. Expected outflow = probability × balance. Annualized fee exposure = expected outflow × annual fee rate.

Member assets are not business revenue. A $100,000 transfer at a 0.5% fee represents $500 annual fee run-rate exposure conditional on exit. Probability-weighting answers a different question from “what if this member leaves?”

What if the business does nothing?

The pooled synthetic test cohorts hold $188,507,565 in total assets. Estimated 90-day outflow is $19,294,569; the associated annualized fee-revenue exposure is $114,992. The balance that would leave if every flagged member departed is $64,625,425; this is a conditional scenario, not expected loss.

The 2,400 holdout snapshots span July–December 2025 and represent different members. Summed exposure is a pooled cohort result, not a single-date current fund forecast. Annualized fee exposure is not a 90-day revenue loss.

Evidence recruiters can inspect

Temporal separation with 90-day maturity gaps between base fitting, calibration, validation and test. Logistic regression selected by validation Brier; threshold 0.10 selected by validation F2.

ROC AUC 0.775; average precision 0.332 against 0.108 prevalence. Recall 70.8%, precision 22.8%. The model catches 184 of 260 simulated leavers but flags 623 members who stay. This tradeoff is reported openly.

Retention investment, with explicit assumptions

Under a 25% save rate among otherwise leavers, one preserved fee year, 70% margin and $25 all-in contact cost, 142 positive-value flagged members are selected against a 200-contact ceiling.

Expected assets retained: $1,668,897. Expected fee revenue preserved: $11,023. Expected net contribution: $4,166.

These are scenario estimates. At capacity 200 all 142 eligible members are contacted, so value, risk and random policies tie. Reducing capacity to 50 makes ranking matter; the notebook and dashboard show that comparison.
